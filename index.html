Methodology & Governance

ICER, discounting and net monetary benefit

ICER = ΔCost / ΔQALY, where ΔCost is net price minus comparator cost and ΔQALY is annual utility gain accumulated over the modelled life years. Following the NICE reference case, QALYs are discounted at 3.5% a year; 1.5% is accepted only exceptionally. The course price is paid up front; ongoing administration and monitoring costs are discounted, and carer QALYs are added without severity weighting. Net monetary benefit is threshold × weighted QALYs minus ΔCost; a positive value means the technology is cost-effective.

Cost-effectiveness thresholds

From April 2026 NICE applies £25,000 to £35,000 per QALY in technology appraisals, up from £20,000 to £30,000. Highly Specialised Technologies use £100,000 per QALY, rising toward £300,000 as lifetime QALY gains grow from 10 to 30; this model scales the weight linearly over that range.

Severity weighting

Severity modifiers multiply the incremental QALYs: 1.2x for an absolute shortfall of 12 to 18 QALYs or a proportional shortfall of 85% to 95%, and 1.7x at 18 QALYs or 95% and above. The higher of the two weights applies, using discounted QALYs. The modifier adjusts the ICER rather than the threshold itself.

Confidential Patient Access Schemes

A PAS lowers the net price paid by the NHS without changing the list price, usually confidentially. The model therefore reports the break-even discount: the smallest discount at which the ICER meets the effective threshold.

NHS England Budget Impact Test

If expected net spend exceeds £40 million in any of a technology's first three years, NHS England may open commercial discussions. The threshold was doubled from £20 million under the 2025 Commercial Framework for New Medicines. Uptake is ramped over five years at 20%, 50%, 80%, 100% and 100% of peak.

VPAG rebate and probabilistic analysis

Company revenue is shown after the 2026 VPAG newer-medicines payment of 14.5% plus the 1% investment programme payment. The acceptability curve draws 1,000 samples varying life years (±30%), utility gain (±25%) and comparator cost (±25%) around the selected values.

Managed access and company valuation

Where a technology is not recommended but shows plausible potential for cost-effectiveness, NICE can recommend use within the Cancer Drugs Fund or Innovative Medicines Fund while evidence is collected. The model flags this when at least 10% of simulated draws are cost-effective, which is a modelling heuristic and not a NICE rule. Valuation applies the modelled approval probability to twelve years of post-VPAG contribution, with revenue falling 70% once exclusivity ends, and excludes R&D and overheads. NHS funding is generally required within 90 days of final guidance.
